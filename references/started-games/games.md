# The four games and the engine contract

This is the long-form reference for everything playable in Arcade Vault: the four catalog engines, the contract they all honour, and the player shell around them. `CLAUDE.md` keeps only the summary and links here — when you touch a game, read this file first.

The prototypes that were ported live next to this one: `02-asteroids/`, `03-tetris/`, `04-arkanoid/`, `05-buscaminas/`. Each keeps its own `CLAUDE.md` and `README.md` describing the original, standalone version. They are not compiled and not deployed; they exist so a ported rule can be checked against its source.

## The catalog

**All four catalog games are real — the five decorative ones from the original mockup are gone (SPEC 17).** Each one is a pure rules module in `lib/` (no `document`, no `window`, no canvas) plus a client component in `components/` that owns the canvas, the `requestAnimationFrame` loop, the keyboard and the on-screen pad.

- **`TETRIX`** (SPEC 13, `lib/tetris.ts` + `components/tetris-game.tsx`) replaced `BLOQUE BUSTER` and `CAÍDA` with a single playable entry, ported from `references/started-games/03-tetris/`: 10 × 20 board, the seven standard tetrominoes, wall kicks `[0, ±1, ±2]`, ghost piece, `NEXT` preview, soft and hard drop, `min(10, floor(lines / 10) + 1)` for the level and `max(100, 1000 − (level − 1) × 90)` ms for the fall — so the level caps at 10 and the interval at 190 ms. One life: the top-out ends the run.
- **`ASTEROIDES`** (SPEC 14, `lib/asteroids.ts` + `components/asteroids-game.tsx`) replaced the decorative `ROCAS` entry — new id, so `/juego/rocas` and `/jugar/rocas` are 404 — and ported `references/started-games/02-asteroids/`: 800 × 600 world that wraps at the edges, ship with inertia, rocks of three sizes worth 20 / 50 / 100 that split in two on each hit, `min(10, 3 + level)` rocks per wave with the level itself uncapped, and two drops, `3x` and shield, never more than one on the field. Four rules depart from the reference on purpose: drag is per second (`0.987 ** 60` raised to `dt`), not per frame; one life instead of three; `fire` is a held state, not an edge, so the on-screen button is playable; and the score has no level multiplier.
- **`ARKANOID`** (SPEC 15, `lib/arkanoid.ts` + `components/arkanoid-game.tsx`) was added as a brand-new catalog entry — not a replacement of a decorative one, unlike `TETRIX` and `ASTEROIDES` — and ports `references/started-games/04-arkanoid/`: 800 × 600 board (exactly the `4 / 3` of `.crt-screen`, so it needs no aspect modifier), 10 × 6 wall of 64 × 24 bricks, three lives, `10 × level` per brick and `100 × level` per cleared wall. Levels are infinite: 1–5 are the reference's five hand-made walls and 6 onward are generated by a deterministic LCG copied straight into `lib/arkanoid.ts` — never imported from elsewhere — seeded with the level number, so they are pure, vertically symmetric and never under 8 bricks. Four rules depart from the reference on purpose: the paddle bounce takes its angle from the impact point (capped at 60°) instead of only flipping `vy`, so the player can aim; ball speed is a two-slope curve from 224 px/s capped at 800; `step()` splits the advance into 8 px substeps and clamps `dt` to 50 ms, so nothing is ever tunneled through; and there is no win state, because the levels do not end.
- **`BUSCAMINAS`** (SPEC 20, `lib/buscaminas.ts` + `components/buscaminas-game.tsx`) is the fourth entry, purely additive like `ARKANOID`, ported from `references/started-games/05-buscaminas/`: fixed 16 × 12 grid of 36 px cells (576 × 432, so `.crt-screen` takes the `minas` modifier), one life, and `minesForLevel = min(60, 9 + level)` — 10 mines at level 1, one more per level, capped from level 51 on. Mines are placed on the **first `reveal()` of each grid**, excluding the clicked cell and its eight neighbours, so the opening click is never fatal. Three rules depart from the reference on purpose: the level progression is new — clearing a grid bumps the level and reseeds a fresh board with one more mine, leaving score and life untouched, so the run is endless; the score is `revealedCells × 10 × level`, not a flat 10 per cell; and the grid is playable by **mouse as well as by cursor** — `onPointerMove` feeds `setCursor`, the primary button reveals, the secondary flags with `contextmenu` prevented, and both input paths share the single `state.cursor`. Like the other three it draws on a canvas inside a `requestAnimationFrame` loop, not a DOM grid — the loop has no `tick()` because nothing falls: it only repaints and drives key repeat.

## The `ENGINES` registry

`components/game-player.tsx` decides which one mounts through the `ENGINES` registry — `{ Component, screen }` keyed by `game.id` (the game's `slug`), where `screen` is the modifier added to `.crt-screen` and is empty when the engine already fits the tube's `4 / 3` (as `ARKANOID` does). The registry holds four rows today — `tetrix`/`tetris`, `asteroides`/`rocks`, `arkanoid`/`""`, `buscaminas`/`minas` — so adding a fifth engine means adding a row there, not another branch, plus a fifth row in `public.games`, since the catalog itself now lives in Supabase. Vidas and the level cap are no longer JS constants either (SPEC 18): `game-player.tsx` reads `game.vidas`/`game.niveles` from that same row and passes them to whichever engine mounts as `initialLives`/`maxLevel`, part of the shared `EngineProps` contract even though only `TETRIX` actually caps a level — `ASTEROIDES`, `ARKANOID` and `BUSCAMINAS` have `niveles: null` (no cap) by design, and `components/buscaminas-game.tsx` declares a structurally identical local `BuscaminasGameProps` and leaves `maxLevel` undestructured on purpose. `LIVES`/`MAX_LEVEL` no longer exist as exports of `lib/tetris.ts`/`lib/asteroids.ts`/`lib/arkanoid.ts`; `lib/buscaminas.ts` still exports a `LIVES = 1` that nothing imports — dead weight, not a second source of truth, and the rule stands: lives come from the row.

## Two rules that keep the engines honest

**The HUD is common to all four games** — player, score, hearts, level, and the `PAUSA` / `FIN` / `SALIR` buttons — so nothing game-specific goes into it and no pause control goes inside the screen; an engine only reports its numbers through `onRun`. What a game may draw inside its own canvas is its own state: `ASTEROIDES` paints the countdown of the active drop, never the score, the lives or the level, `ARKANOID` paints nothing but the board, and `BUSCAMINAS` nothing but the grid and its cursor.

And **saving a score is real, but conditional** (SPEC 18): `game-player.tsx` calls `increment_game_plays()` on mount so `games.plays` counts every real attempt regardless of outcome, and separately, once the run ends (real game-over or the `FIN` button) `GUARDAR PUNTUACIÓN` calls the `save_score` RPC — only offered, and only wired to do anything, when the result beats the player's own best for that game; a non-record run shows "TU MEJOR MARCA … SIGUE SIENDO …" instead, with no request to the server at all. The modal shows the session's name — no editable input — and a guest gets an invitation to sign in instead of the save button: it sends the run to `/auth` and back through the URL (`/jugar/tetrix?puntuacion=&nivel=`), which `app/jugar/[id]/page.tsx` validates and hands to the player as `restored` — and, if it's still a record once a real session exists, the run auto-saves without another click.

## Fullscreen, mobile only (SPEC 19)

A `⛶` toggle drives the real Fullscreen API over the whole `.av-player`. Two details depart from what the spec text proposed, and the code is the truth: the button **floats absolutely inside `.crt`** (`components/game-player.tsx:308-321`), not in `.hud-actions`, because at 390px `PAUSA`/`FIN`/`SALIR` already fill that row — and because `.av-player:fullscreen .hud-actions { display: none }` (`app/globals.css:1216-1218`) hides those three in fullscreen, which makes the floating button the only way out; and support is detected with `useSyncExternalStore` (`getServerSnapshot` returns `false`), not a `useEffect`, so the server HTML and the first client render agree. A `fullscreenchange` listener keeps `isFullscreen` honest against Esc and the Android back gesture, and unmounting the player exits if it was still active. CSS shows the button only under 720px (`app/globals.css:2283-2285`). There is no orientation lock, by decision.

## Adding a fifth game

Deciding _which_ game comes first, and that is the **`game-planner`** subagent
(`.agents/agents/game-planner.md`): it recomputes the catalog's gaps from the migrations, runs candidates
through the eight hard gates the contract above implies, returns a ranked three with one recommendation,
and records every idea — rejections included — in [`suggestions-games.md`](suggestions-games.md), which
is its memory across runs. It writes no spec and no code; its last line is always `/add-game <TÍTULO>`.

Then the **`/add-game`** skill: it front-loads the contract above (common HUD, `EngineProps`, the `ENGINES` registry, the `games`/`categorias` columns), asks only about what is genuinely undecided for that game, and writes a spec — never code. SPEC 20 came out of it.

Where the pieces land: a pure module in `lib/`, a `"use client"` component in `components/`, a row in `ENGINES`, a `.crt-screen` modifier and its CSS in `app/globals.css`, an additive `insert into public.games` under `supabase/migrations/`, a cover image in `public/juegos/`, and a `describe` block in `tests/screens.spec.ts` that does not freeze the clock — the loops need a live `requestAnimationFrame` — and asserts nothing that depends on the randomness of a run.
